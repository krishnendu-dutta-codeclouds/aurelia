Kakadu Plum: The World's Richest Natural Source of Vitamin C

The Kakadu Plum contains up to 5,300mg of Vitamin C per 100g — over 100× more than an orange. Here is the science behind this extraordinary Australian native fruit.

L

Léa Fontaine

Clinical Aesthetician

Terminalia ferdinandiana — the Kakadu plum — is a small deciduous tree native to the tropical woodlands of northern Australia. For thousands of years, it has been used by Aboriginal Australians as both food and medicine. Modern phytochemical analysis reveals why: it contains the highest recorded natural concentration of Vitamin C of any food source on Earth.

The Numbers

- Fresh Kakadu plum pulp: 2,907–5,300 mg ascorbic acid per 100g - Orange: 59 mg per 100g - Rosehip: 400–600 mg per 100g

This extraordinary concentration is not incidental. The tree evolved under intense UV radiation in the tropical north — high Vitamin C production is a biological adaptation to combat photodamage at the cellular level.

Beyond Vitamin C

While the ascorbic acid content is the headline, Kakadu plum's full phytochemical profile includes: - Ellagic acid and punicalin (powerful polyphenol antioxidants) - Gallic acid (anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial) - Lutein (photoprotective carotenoid) - Calcium, magnesium, and trace minerals

This synergistic complex likely amplifies the efficacy of the vitamin C fraction — a phenomenon known as the 'entourage effect' in botanical chemistry.

Sourcing Ethics

We source our Kakadu plum extract exclusively from an Aboriginal-owned cooperative in the Northern Territory. This is not a marketing decision — it is an ethical obligation. These communities have cultivated and stewarded this plant for millennia; they deserve to be the primary economic beneficiaries of its commercialisation.

Our supplier holds Fair Trade certification and the Indigenous Land Use Agreement, and we pay a 12% premium above market rate that is directed entirely to community infrastructure projects.

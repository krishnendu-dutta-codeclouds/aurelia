3:T8a9,Vitamin C — ascorbic acid — is one of the most extensively studied topical antioxidants in dermatological science. Yet despite decades of research, it remains one of the most misunderstood ingredients on the market.

**The Stability Problem**

Pure L-ascorbic acid is inherently unstable. Exposed to air, light, or water, it oxidises rapidly — turning from a transparent serum into a discoloured, pro-oxidant compound that can actually *damage* skin instead of protecting it. This is the fundamental challenge every formulation chemist faces.

Most brands solve this problem by adding chelating agents and keeping the pH below 3.5 — the range at which ascorbic acid remains relatively stable. The downside: pH 3.5 is deeply acidic, and many skin types — especially sensitised, rosacea-prone, or melanin-rich skin — cannot tolerate it without irritation.

**Our Solution: Tetrahexyldecyl Ascorbate**

Tetrahexyldecyl Ascorbate (THDC) is an oil-soluble, esterified form of Vitamin C. Unlike its water-soluble counterpart, THDC does not require an acidic pH environment to remain stable — it stays active at physiological pH (5.0–6.5) and resists oxidation for significantly longer.

More importantly, THDC is lipid-soluble, meaning it penetrates the stratum corneum far more efficiently than L-ascorbic acid. Once inside the epidermis, it is enzymatically converted back to ascorbic acid exactly where it needs to work.

**Clinical Implications**

In an independent double-blind trial conducted across 62 participants over 12 weeks, THDC at 15% concentration demonstrated:
- 38% reduction in surface melanin density
- 44% improvement in measured luminosity (L* value, CIELAB)
- 0 reported cases of contact sensitivity or irritation

These numbers explain why we built the Aura Radiance Serum around THDC as its cornerstone active.

**Pairing for Maximum Efficacy**

THDC performs best when paired with Ferulic Acid (which doubles its antioxidant capacity) and Niacinamide (which suppresses melanin transfer without the instability risks of other brightening agents).

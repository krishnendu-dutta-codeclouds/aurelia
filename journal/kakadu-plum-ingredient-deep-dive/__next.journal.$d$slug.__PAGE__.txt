3:T6cc,Terminalia ferdinandiana — the Kakadu plum — is a small deciduous tree native to the tropical woodlands of northern Australia. For thousands of years, it has been used by Aboriginal Australians as both food and medicine. Modern phytochemical analysis reveals why: it contains the highest recorded natural concentration of Vitamin C of any food source on Earth.

**The Numbers**

- Fresh Kakadu plum pulp: 2,907–5,300 mg ascorbic acid per 100g
- Orange: 59 mg per 100g
- Rosehip: 400–600 mg per 100g

This extraordinary concentration is not incidental. The tree evolved under intense UV radiation in the tropical north — high Vitamin C production is a biological adaptation to combat photodamage at the cellular level.

**Beyond Vitamin C**

While the ascorbic acid content is the headline, Kakadu plum's full phytochemical profile includes:
- Ellagic acid and punicalin (powerful polyphenol antioxidants)
- Gallic acid (anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial)
- Lutein (photoprotective carotenoid)
- Calcium, magnesium, and trace minerals

This synergistic complex likely amplifies the efficacy of the vitamin C fraction — a phenomenon known as the 'entourage effect' in botanical chemistry.
